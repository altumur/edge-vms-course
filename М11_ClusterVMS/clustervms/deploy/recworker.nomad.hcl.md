# recworker.nomad.hcl — the recorder job: the only one placed on the archive for footage

**Role.** Lesson 2 (the shape) and Lesson 4 (its failover). `service`, `count = N` with the worker's `scaling` block on `avg(rec_worker_load)` plus one of its own on `rec_volumes_unserved`, the archive constraint `meta.archive is_set` — the line that used to be the worker's reason for the archive and is the recorder's now — `spread` over `node.unique.id`, the same `disconnect` numbers and `kill_timeout = 20s` (SIGTERM finalizes the open segment; the last pass promotes it). Volumes: the spool and the archive — it is the only writer of segments — `/run/vms` (the workers' shared-memory branches on this server), and no `/data/media`: it never reads a camera. The rec spec has no `near`: a recorder is pinned to disks and cannot follow anything, so the recording names its server (`home` on its row) and the camera comes to it (`near: rec` in the vms spec). The usual path is therefore `shmsrc`, not RTSP — a preference, never a filter. `network_mode = host`: it subscribes to workers' RTSP fan-outs on any server. `ARCHIVE = ${meta.archive}` is handed to the process as for the worker.

## Notes
- Both scaling checks need a Prometheus to read, and this directory does not deploy one (see `autoscaler.nomad.hcl.md`; М13 builds it). Without it the job runs at whatever `count` says and the unserved archives stay visible on the console — the check is automation, not a dependency of the recorder.
- Two scaling checks, because a recorder has two demands and `load` sees only one. A recorder holds ONE archive (`servers: distinct` over `place_by: volume`), so an archive that is declared (`rec/volumes/*`) and held by nobody needs a PROCESS — while every existing recorder may be perfectly loaded. `rec_volumes_unserved` is published by the console beside `rec_worker_load`; the Autoscaler takes the larger of the counts the checks compute, and the archive check never asks for fewer than are running, so scale-in stays `load`'s alone.
- `rec_worker_load` deliberately leaves out SPARES — recorders holding no volume. A spare reports zero capacity, which the formula would read as fully loaded, and one spare would then demand the next for ever. They are counted as `rec_spare_workers` instead.
- `rec/policy {servers: distinct}` by default: a rescheduled `r-1` beside `r-2` on one server idles by policy; the rec controller moves a dead server's recordings instead (two silences). The administrator may set `shared` from the page's `/rec/policy`.
- Bound policy: `recworker-policy.hcl` — `rec/epoch/*`, `rec/slots/*`, its heartbeat under `objects/rec/*`; reads `vms/*` and `objects/vms/*` for the workers' `live_url`.
